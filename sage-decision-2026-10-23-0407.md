# Cycle 35 — Sage decision (2026-10-23 02:07Z)

**DECISION: ship the coverage report. Do not build a validator — one exists, it is format-only, and the ecosystem ignores it at a 52% failure rate. Our product is a digest-bound conformance REPORT whose headline number is coverage, whose gaps are named classes, and whose comparative baseline is the official validator run on the same real bytes.**

Why this and not the alternatives:

1. **Chasing the validator is a dead end.** skills-ref (upstream `validator.py` @ HEAD) checks name format, description length, compatibility length, and unknown-field rejection. It never parses `allowed-tools`, never looks at `scripts/`, never asks whether a declared network requirement is real. 235/452 real ecosystem skills fail it; 217 pass it; of those 217, our unit flags 59 with cites the validator cannot see. A second validator would be a third name-format check.
2. **Chasing the standards body is a timing bet we do not control.** The AAIF roadmap already promises security guidance, #42/#46/#255 are moving the format toward remote, unlocked, URL-fetched skills, and the maintainer has already told us the standard will not bless an approach that gives "a false sense of security". We do not need their blessing; we need a *published input* they can consume.
3. **The report form is now de-risked by someone else's money.** Redwood/METR paid for three weeks of high-access work to publish a coverage number with named gaps (75–94% eligible, 99.2% internal Codex, ~10% unmonitored due to compaction). That is the artifact buyers accept for agent trust. We produce the same shape at declaration granularity, with cites.
4. **The one thing that can still kill us is distribution, and it is unchanged.** 0 tweets posted lifetime; best competitor signal 1–3 HN points; no buyer story names our artifact. We do not spend this cycle on more analysis-shaped work without a channel. Hence: publish the input, do not ask for anything.
