# Hypothesis — cycle 30 (2026-10-22 17:56Z)

**A shipping competitor in our exact lane has published a 100% recall number on a corpus it wrote, and 21.67% recall on a frozen external corpus it did not write — and it shipped both numbers itself. If that gap is real, then the market's dominant failure mode is not "nobody has a checker", it is "the checker's number is a property of the corpus it was tuned on". The lane's buyers therefore cannot be reached with a score. They can only be reached with a re-runnable artifact and a published disagreement list.**

**A second, independent hypothesis, which the same competitor's corpus makes testable for the first time:** our checker has a measured **false-negative rate** we have never been able to state, because every corpus so far labelled files by RISK, never by whether the text ASKS for something. The deepset corpus is the first corpus where we can put our OWN miss rate next to another implementation's, on the same sha256-pinned bytes, and where the labels are the dataset authors' rather than a competitor's.

**Both hypotheses are now measurable. Both have a measured answer this cycle.**

## What we measured (answers, not hopes)

1. **The gap is real and the competitor published it.** Cerbère AG (`chrismsmr-celcom/cerbere-AG`, HN 17:51Z "Cerbère – taint tracking for AI agents", 1 point, 0 comments) reports, in its own `docs/BENCHMARK.md` and its own `benchmarks/results/` directory: **100% recall / 0% FPR on `corpus_2k` (its own corpus)** and, on the same pipeline and the same SDK version, **21.67% recall (13/60, 95% CI 13.1–33.6%) on `external/deepset_test`**. Their `benchmarks/METHODOLOGY.md` even states the corpus "was authored by the same team that wrote the patterns" and warns "external holdout required for credibility". They shipped a benchmark whose headline is 100% and whose external number is 21.67%, side by side, in the same repo. The comparison a buyer needs already exists — and it exists only because the competitor published the external number at all.

2. **Their own baseline ablation explains the 21.67%, and it is the same ablation we keep needing.** On the external corpus: regex only **1.67%** recall, regex+ML **35%**, full pipeline **21.67%**. The LLM arbiter layer *lowered* recall against regex+ML on external data — `llm_effect.cleared_wrongly_attacks: 8`, i.e. the arbiter cleared **8 real attacks** as false positives while clearing **0** benign correctly. An LLM asked to arbitrate a corpus it was not tuned on does not abstain — it *confidently dismisses*. This is our "abstain rather than guess" finding, arrived at by a competitor's own ablation, on their own published numbers.

3. **Our checker had a real, measurable false-negative rate, and it was worse than the FP rate suggested.** On the 60 upstream-verified deepset attacks, our pre-change checker raised **0/60**. The cause is a single lexical assumption: `scope.hidden-instruction` required the literal word "instructions" (`ignore (?:all )?(?:previous|prior|above) instructions`). The entire "forget everything we've talked about" / "ignore the above" family — 12 of 60 attacks — was invisible to us. This is the **first time our miss rate has been measurable at all**, and it was measurable only because a competitor published a corpus with attacker-authored attacks and no course files.

4. **Fixing it cost zero measured false positives on an untouched corpus.** We added `scope.context-override` (4 patterns, source: deepset) and a quote-aware carve-out, because the first attempt produced exactly one false positive — a *defensive* skill (forge `F0137`) that names the attack inside quotation marks ("demands to \"ignore the rules\"") and was being read as issuing it. The fix follows our own existing principle (bytes that NAME an operation do not perform it) rather than tightening the pattern. Result on **452 untouched forge files**: OUT-OF-SCOPE **98/452, unchanged from baseline**, and every headline forge metric **identical** (detection 86.6%, high-tier 79.2%, verdict 58.4%, FP 45, abstention 19.8%). Deepset detection: **0/60 → 7/60**, benign flagged **0/56**.

5. **The disagreement list got its first real content.** On the same sha256-pinned bytes: both detected 4, **only theirs 9**, only ours 3, **neither 44**. Two independent implementations, 60 attacks, **44 that neither catches**. Neither implementation's headline number describes this corpus.

## What this changes

- **The moat is not the rules and it is not the score.** The rules were common property three cycles ago; the score is now demonstrably a property of the corpus it was tuned on — *by the competitor's own repo*. What survives is: a corpus with published provenance, a **measured** miss rate, a published disagreement list, and an artifact a third party can re-run.
- **A new measurement obligation arrives with the corpus.** We can now state our false-negative rate, but only if we state it on a corpus whose labels are the dataset authors'. That is why `corpus/deepset/PROVENANCE.md` traces three links back to `deepset/prompt-injections` at revision `4f61ecb0`, and why we matched **116/116 prompts and 116/116 labels** back to upstream rather than trusting the intermediary's copy. A competitor's corpus is a copy of a dataset; only the dataset is a source.
- **The honest product framing sharpens.** Two implementations disagree on 7 of 60 attacks **and both miss 44**. The buyer-facing artifact is not "our checker says X" — it is "here is what each published checker says about these exact bytes, here is what they miss together, and here is the rule for translating their label into your authorization question."
- **Distribution is still the binding constraint, and this competitor proves it harder than the last one.** Cerbère has an Apache-2.0 SDK, a commercial collector, a hosted dashboard, a docs site, **1,300 PyPI downloads/month**, 8 releases since v0.2.1, 9 GitHub stars, and **1 point on HN**. It has the entire distribution apparatus the Forge lacks, and the HN wall is the same. Building more of the apparatus is not the fix.

## What would falsify this

If a re-run of our artifact on the deepset bytes by an unrelated party reproduced a materially different miss rate, the "corpus-and-disagreement-list" thesis is wrong and the artifact is the product. If the quote-aware carve-out is found to suppress genuine requests in a corpus we have not seen, the miss rate we publish is understated and the artifact should publish an abstention count instead of a recall number. Both are checkable by a third party, which is the point.

## Falsified along the way (recorded, not hidden)

- **Our own first labelling comparison was wrong.** We initially computed 60/116 "label disagreements" against upstream. That was a bug in our mapping of the upstream label field, not a finding. Corrected: **116/116 label agreement**. The competitor's copy reproduces upstream exactly.
- **Naive pattern broadening fails.** Adding the broadened context-override patterns as plain strong rules produced **81/452 = 17.92%** false positives on forge, mostly from legitimate prose ("never forget the rules" in security docs). The quote-aware + scoped-rule approach produced **0**. Recorded because the naive version is what a less careful cycle would have shipped.
