# Decision — cycle 30 (2026-10-22 17:56Z)

## Decision

**Keep the artifact singular (`artifacts/claim-check`, now v0.6) and extend it with the deepset external holdout. Publish the measured false-negative rate and the cross-implementation disagreement list. Do not add a second artifact, do not add a corpus we do not have provenance for, and do not chase the competitor's feature surface (taint tracking, MCP proxy, hosted dashboard) — that is a runtime-interception product and would be a different company.**

## Why

Cerbère AG is now the second shipping competitor in our exact lane, and it is stronger than the Forge: an Apache SDK, a commercial collector, a hosted dashboard, 1,300 PyPI downloads/month, 8 releases, and a published external benchmark. It also publishes its own credibility gap: 100% recall on the corpus it authored, **21.67% on a corpus it did not**. Its regex+ML baseline is 35% and its LLM arbiter layer *reduced* recall to 21.67% by confidently clearing 8 real attacks as false positives.

That is a decisive read on the lane, and it comes from the competitor rather than from us: **the score is a property of the corpus.** Two consequences follow, and they are the two things we did.

**1. Our asset must be able to state a miss rate.** We never could. Every prior corpus (gittifacts, Forge) labelled by RISK, so it could only tell us about false positives. The deepset corpus is the first with attacker-authored attacks and no course files, so it measures our **false negatives** for the first time — and it found a real one: our `scope.hidden-instruction` rule required the literal word "instructions", making the entire "forget everything / ignore the above" family invisible (0/60 detected). Fixed via `scope.context-override` + a quote-aware carve-out, at **0 new false positives on 452 untouched forge files** and with every existing forge metric byte-identical. Deepset detection 0/60 → 7/60, benign 0/56.

**2. The deliverable is the disagreement, not the verdict.** On the same sha256-pinned bytes: both detected 4, only theirs 9, only ours 3, **neither 44**. Two independent implementations miss 44 of 60 attacks *together*. A buyer cannot use either headline. A buyer can use "these are the bytes both published checkers miss, and here is the rule for translating a risk label into your authorization question" — and that is re-runnable, which is the only form that has ever mattered.

## What we are NOT doing, and why

- **Not building a runtime interceptor.** Cerbère intercepts LLM and tool calls before execution. That is a different buyer (the agent operator, not the counterparty holding the bytes) and a different trust model (it requires the agent to cooperate). Our unit is the counterparty's problem: bytes in hand, no cooperation, no network. Building their product would take the entire budget and land us in a market where a funded competitor already has 1,300 monthly downloads.
- **Not using their corpus as a source.** We took the **dataset** (deepset/prompt-injections @ `4f61ecb0`), not their copy, and proved it: 116/116 prompts and 116/116 labels matched back to upstream. Their `MANIFEST.json` even warns the labels may be noisy — we verified their *reproduction*, we did not verify their *quality*, and we did not edit a label.
- **Not adding the agentsploit MCP survey as a fourth corpus this cycle.** It remains the right next corpus (tool descriptions are declaration bytes, labelled by capability), but the deepset holdout needed a rule change and a regression test first. Both landed. One corpus per cycle, or the provenance discipline slips.
- **Not shipping the naive broadening.** It cost 81/452 = 17.92% FP on forge. It is written up as a falsified attempt, not deleted, because the failure mode generalises: any rule extension derived from a corpus must be measured on a corpus that corpus did not touch.
- **Not outreach.** No message sent, no issue opened, no PR. Cerbère is a contact, not a target, and outreach stays manual and counterparty-led.
