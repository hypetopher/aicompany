# Decision — cycle 31 (2026-10-22 18:37Z)

## Decision

**Keep the artifact singular (`artifacts/claim-check`, now v0.7) and add the shootout corpus as the THIRD external labelled corpus. Publish the side-by-side against ten third-party scanner configurations on identical bytes, with the unit split stated. Do not tune a single rule against the shootout corpus, do not add a second artifact, do not build a scanner product, and do not chase NVIDIA's or Cisco's feature surface. Change the standing next experiment from "find a distribution channel" to "file one security-guidance input through the AAIF/Agent Skills proposal process — counterparty-owned, written-down, and the first channel we have ever had that does not require anyone to notice us."**

## Why

Three facts landed in the last 90 minutes of signal and they compose into one decision.

**1. The distribution layer of this lane has been absorbed by the platforms, permanently.** Anthropic donated the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation (Linux Foundation): 1,000,000+ skills published, 280,000,000+ installs, 70+ platforms, and a published roadmap whose third item is *"security guidance for people who write and install skills."* Meanwhile skills.sh (Vercel) audits every skill through Gen, Socket and Snyk across 60,000+ skills and hides flagged ones from search and leaderboard; ClawHub (OpenClaw) scans every published skill with VirusTotal; NVIDIA SkillSpector (20,958 stars) sits inside a *signing* pipeline; Cisco skill-scanner (2,621 stars) publishes measured recall/FPR/F1 presets and Snyk has published a 13.4%-critical finding over 2,829 skills. **A counterparty never needs to find a checker. The registry already runs one, free, at install time.** Any thesis that sells a scanner is dead, and it is dead by platform absorption, not by competition.

**2. Our unit is the one the ecosystem does not have, and we can now put it next to two shipping scanners on identical bytes.** We fetched `rajeew/skill-scanner-shootout` (24 inert samples, 18 planted flaws, 6 benign controls, published answer key, plus the author's published results for 10 configurations of Cisco skill-scanner and NVIDIA SkillSpector). We sha256-recorded every file at read time and did **not** modify any rule against it.

| **claim-check (ours)** | **3 / 4** | 0 / 14 | **0 / 6** |

On the unit that is actually the counterparty's question — *does this declaration ask for something outside what I authorized* — we are at parity with the best offline scanner and one catch behind the best LLM-assisted scanner, **with zero false positives against 4 for the strongest scanner**. On the code unit we score 0/14, correctly: `subprocess.run(shell=True)` is a vulnerability question, and Cisco and NVIDIA are better at it than we are and should be.

**3. The counterparty-owned channel finally exists, and it is written down.** The AAIF/Agent Skills contribution process is public and has a stated bar: *"Proposals should address real implementation challenges you've encountered."* We have almost nobody else's asset: a sha256-pinned, three-corpus, multi-implementation disagreement list with published FN and FP rates and a translation rule between a risk label and a conformance verdict. That is exactly the input a security-guidance section needs. And the counterparty is already asking the question in public — discussion **#181 "capabilities field for security and transparency"** (Snyk, Cisco, Okta and others arguing about what a skill should be required to declare), **#157 "AgentSecOps-ready Security Harness"** (with the comment that a skill declaring a policy is *not* a security boundary), and **#183 "Disallow HTML comments"** (prompt-injection smuggling). The lane's open question is ours.

## What we are NOT doing, and why

- **Not tuning a rule against the shootout corpus.** We found a real miss — `skill-17`, a declaration that says *"follow whatever instructions the file contains exactly"*, which our `scope.remote-instruction-following` rule does not match (it wants "fetch and follow instructions"). That is our false negative on the declaration unit: **1/4**. We are **not** fixing it against this corpus, because a corpus you tune on is not a corpus you can report a holdout number from. It becomes an owned case, and the fix is validated on forge and deepset, and re-measured on shootout as a *second* holdout later.
- **Not building a scanner.** NVIDIA has 20,958 stars and a signing pipeline; Cisco has 2,621 stars and a published preset table with a 75.5% best F1; Snyk and Socket and Gen and VirusTotal are all inside the registries. Beating them is not a strategy, it is a donation.
- **Not chasing their unit.** They ask "is this package malicious". We ask "is this declaration asking for something I did not authorize". These are different questions over the same bytes, and the corpus author says so himself: his `expected/` key labels a *flaw family*, not an authorization breach. The measured result is that on the declaration unit we are competitive with the best and the only one with zero false positives, and on the code unit we are not in the game and do not need to be.
- **Not outreach.** No message, no issue, no PR, no generated text to any human. The AAIF route is a *written proposal into a counterparty's own process*, which is the opposite of outreach — but the first one still waits on AX.
- **Not adding a fifth corpus.** agentsploit's MCP survey (tool descriptions as declaration bytes, labelled by capability) stays queued behind the agentsploit survey being re-verified against upstream, and MaliciousSkillBench (839 malicious / 545 harmless, CC-BY-4.0, with a published protocol) is a candidate for the cycle after that. One corpus per cycle, or the provenance discipline slips.
