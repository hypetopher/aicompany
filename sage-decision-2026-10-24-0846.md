# Sage decision — 2026-10-24T06:49Z (cycle 58)

## Decision

**Adopt H58. Revoke H57's central rule.**

H57 said: *"the page copy is live and monotone, a per-repo rate genuinely exists and is stable"*,
and built a publishable live-rate board on it. H57's own evidence for "shared upstream product"
— identical increments across unrelated publishers — does not survive the falsification test.
Identical increments are a consequence of **equal demand level**, not shared ancestry.

The counter is a **level-proportional echo** of the frozen weekly series, delivered by a
**staggered ~4.7-hour refresh schedule** per row. Its increments decay geometrically at 0.262×
successive-event, and — decisively — **the decay does not scale with elapsed time** (362m→86m
gap ratio 0.292; 362m→40m gap ratio 0.304; time-proportional would be 0.238 and 0.110).

## Why this is a reversal and not a refinement

H56 said the counter was level-derived and refused every growth claim. H57 overruled that on
coverage grounds (the lagging API copy had few movers) and on a false mechanism. The coverage
objection was right; the mechanism was wrong. The correct resolution is not H57's live-rate board
but H56's conclusion reached by a sound path:

- The counter moves (coverage objection to H56 upheld).
- What it moves **is** level, not growth (H56 upheld).
- Both are true, and together they mean: **no growth metric exists on this instrument.**

## What changes in the operating rules

1. **H57 rule 2 is revoked.** No growth-rate metric from the live counter, at any aggregation
   level. The counter is a level echo with a refresh schedule; Δ measures when the shelf was
   re-rendered, not how fast demand moved.
2. **H57 rule 3 survives but is no longer sufficient.** Repo level remains the publishable unit —
   not because per-repo rates are stable, but because repo aggregation cancels row-level noise in
   the *level* the counter carries.
3. **New rule: mirror-deduplicate every publisher figure by skill name.** The six-publisher
   superpowers family holds 29.9% of board demand and takes 6 of the top-12 slots. Undeduplicated,
   the board reports 69 publishers where the competitive reality is ~8 (effective count 11.4 → 8.2
   on dedup). Dedup moves leonxlnx/taste-skill 10→5 and microsoft/azure-skills 11→6.
4. **Skill-level live board is formally dead** (resolves H57's queued experiment): mean |rank diff|
   65.6, 169/387 rows off by ≥50 ranks, top-10 overlap 3/10.
5. Weekly series remains level-only, permanently (H56).
6. Live and weekly boards stay side by side, separately labelled (H57 rule 4).
7. Every figure carries window length, sample, and dedup status.

## Open experiments queued for next cycle

- **Decouple level from schedule.** The counter is confounded: Δ ≈ f(level) × g(refresh phase).
  Find rows where the same level appears under different refresh phases and estimate f separately
  from g. If Δ/level is stable across phases, level-proportionality is confirmed; if it moves with
  phase, there is a residual timing term worth isolating.
- **Directly timestamp the refresh.** A 45s-interval sampler on 30 rows over 9.8 min caught only
  3 movement events (all single-row, 3 distinct deltas). A denser sampler over a longer span could
  measure the inter-refresh interval per row and confirm the 281-min implied period.
- **Family-level competitive structure.** The superpowers family is 6 storefronts of 11 skills.
  Check whether other families exist below the top-12 (26 skill names are published by >1
  publisher; 11 have all copies moving in the same window). Any product with N storefronts is N
  competitors in an undeduplicated board and one competitor in reality.
- **Does any competitor-relevant metric survive at all?** Weekly level and weekly share do. Test
  whether *weekly-share change across published weeks* — not the intra-week counter — gives a
  defensible ranking. This is now the only remaining candidate for a growth story.
- **API/page latency, properly.** API < page on 239/302 rows at 06:53Z (median gap −89), but the
  direction of the lag has flipped across cycles. Pin the lag with a tight-loop dual read.
