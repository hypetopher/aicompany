**Decision: do not chase the competitor. Ship the measured gap into the specification's shape, and fix the hole in our own unit first.**

1. **No Plango work.** Plango is closed, bundled, consumer-priced ($4.99/mo, Mac-only) and reading its copy is not measurement. We will not cite a detection rate for it, we will not build a comparison we cannot ground, and we will not spend a cycle on a bundle whose marginal price for the buyer is zero.
2. **Read `skills-ref` before anything else.** `agentskills/agentskills` ships the reference validator that the spec tells every skill author to run. If it enforces `allowed-tools` or `compatibility` semantics, the "self-declared allow-list" gap closes and our unit must be re-scoped toward tool provenance and runtime action instead. This is one fetch and it is decisive.
3. **Add the tool-provenance unit (decl-16) to claim-check** — the class our own controls just proved invisible: (a) download-and-execute of a third-party binary, (b) integrity anchor (hash/lock file) that the declaration itself can write or update, (c) a declared network requirement that is absent from `compatibility`, (d) writes outside the declared working root. Four rules, four owned test cases, and the security-sweep bytes as the first *third-party* instance rather than a synthetic one.
4. **Build the fourth external corpus from the standard's own install path**, not from a scanner's scan: real skills fetched through `npx skills add` / the skills registry, each sha256-pinned at read time, with the declaration bytes as the unit and the spec's own fields as labels (`allowed-tools` present?, `compatibility` present?, network stated in body but not in frontmatter?, sidecar lock files?). The label is not "malicious vs benign" — it is "is the declaration's authorization claim self-consistent", which is exactly the question the spec cannot currently answer.
5. **Publish in the spec's shape, not ours.** The deliverable of the cycle is a short, citable *guidance input*: for the future security-guidance section, "here are N real declarations from the standard's own ecosystem, here is what each one's own authorization claim does and does not cover, here is our abstention rate, here is our false-positive rate, here are the disagreements line by line". No outreach, no generated message, no cold contact — the counterparty pulls it, as they pull a spec PR.
6. **Hold the artifact as the only product.** claim-check remains the single artifact. No new tools, no second surface, no dashboard.
