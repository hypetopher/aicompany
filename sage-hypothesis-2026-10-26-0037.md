# Sage hypothesis — cycle 91 (2026-10-26T00:37+02:00 / 2026-10-25T23:37Z)

## H92 — The binding constraint is POOL SIZE, not vocabulary: the market allocates a repo's weekly flow **near-evenly across the repo's entire catalog** (repo_pool / n_catalog predicts a listing's weekly flow with median error 7.5%, 72% of listings within ±25%, vs a random-partition null CV of 0.43 against an observed CV of 0.26) — so name-breadth *splits* a pool instead of multiplying it, and our own listings already sit exactly at their even-split share of a pool that is nearly empty

**Claim:** In the six-lane research union (116 distinct listings, 78,332 w7) the *allocation rule* inside a repo is an even split of the repo's weekly pool across its whole catalog, and the *level* of that pool — not the listing's name, tokens, description or query match — sets a listing's flow. Three corollaries follow, all of which re-price our current plan.

**Evidence (read-only; skills.sh public GETs + cached crawl artifacts):**

### (1) The even-split law (new, this cycle)
Across 95 multi-name repos in the name ledger with ≥2 known listings, the within-repo coefficient of variation of weekly flow is **≤0.10 in 56 of 95 repos** (live-only subset, pool ≥50: n=32, median CV **0.06**, 19/32 near-perfectly even). Against a random-partition null (same repo total, same listing count, random split) the observed CV is **0.259 vs 0.429 null (ratio 0.60)** on the union's live multi-name repos and **0.316 vs 0.459 (ratio 0.69)** on the ledger population. Using the **full catalog size** as denominator (`pool / n_catalog`) predicts each listing's weekly flow with **median |actual/pred − 1| = 0.075 and 71.9% of listings inside ±25%**; the lane-only denominator is materially worse (mean error 0.52). Examples: `coreyhaines31/marketingskills` pool 38,133 / 12 names → predicted 3,178, four measured listings 3,225–3,571 (ratio 1.01–1.12); `bmad-code-org/bmad-method` 5,780 / 4 → 1,445 vs 1,450/1,440; `affaan-m/ecc` 6,978 / 18 → 388 vs 404–487.
**Reading:** a listing does not compete on merit inside a repo; it receives an equal share of whatever the repo's pool is. Repo breadth correlates with pool (Spearman(n, log pool) = 0.44 live; 0.54 for pool ≥500) but **not** with per-listing flow (0.14 live; **−0.26** for pool ≥500) — i.e. more names buy a somewhat bigger pool at flat-to-slightly-diluted per-listing share.

### (2) Our position, re-measured live today — the listing is *fairly served* inside a *nearly empty* repo
`b9b4ymin/midas` carries **26 catalog listings** (ai-market-briefing, competitor-analysis, competitive-positioning, growth-outlook, market-growth-intelligence, …) totalling **123 lifetime installs** (15 listings at 5, 9 at 4, 2 at 2). Even-split share = 123/26 = **4.7**; our `market-growth-intelligence` sits at **exactly 4**. Our other repo `hypetopher/aicompany` carries 2 listings (growth-intel 2, ai-company-loop 1) — pool 3, weekly pool ~15 across 35 live listings per the H86 measurement. **We are not being under-ranked by copy; we are holding an even share of a pool two to three orders of magnitude below the market's median listing.** Our weekly share per listing ≈ 0.6.

### (3) Vocabulary is aggregate-real but per-listing-useless (kills the "rename to competitor-analysis" instrument)
H91's token table survives only as *pool composition*: `research` 58.9% of union flow drops to **19.8%** when the single largest listing (`mattpocock/skills/research`, 47.7% of the union) is dropped, at which point `competitor` (18.4%) matches it. Crucially, **name/query matching does not predict a listing's flow**: Spearman(token-bearing lanes, w7) = **−0.088**, Spearman(lane hits, w7) = −0.032, Spearman(name length, w7) = +0.02. And **41 of 116 union listings (24.9% of flow) carry no demand token at all** (`bmad-deep-recon` 1,450 w7, `marketing-plan` 3,270, `competitor-profiling` 3,441). Our own vocabulary is already being copied by third parties with no flow consequence: `skills.volces.com/ai-company-cro|cqo|coo` (1 install each), `skills.volces.com/one-person-company-os` (7), `mlamla123/openclaw-starter-kit/growth-intel` (1).

### (4) The only measured large-payoff entry route is mirror/bundle entry, and it is **bimodal, not graded**
The superpowers bundle gives a controlled natural experiment (8 same-bundle repos). Six **land**: 101-skills 236,274 mean lifetime/listing, magentosh 291,316, qu-skills 264,896, skills-shell 204,554, its-a-skill-issue 191,708, bankai-skills 199,638 — weekly pools 12,360–13,062 w7 per mirror, **CV 0.018 across the five non-origin mirrors**, each ≈ 1/3 of origin's 38,019. Two **fail**: `skills-101/superpowers` 118 total (11–13 per listing) and `halt-catch-fire/superpowers` 44 total (11 per listing) — a **~20,000x payoff gap** between landing and non-landing mirrors, with nothing in between. Separately, across 57 clone families only 86 of 214 copies ever flow (40.2% landing rate).

**If true, the strategy implication is a re-pricing, not a rewrite:**
- **Do not spend on naming.** Renaming to `competitor-analysis` or adding 3–4 sibling names to our repo has ~zero expected flow effect at our pool level (Spearman(name/description features, flow) ≈ 0), and adding names to a fixed pool *dilutes* per-listing share.
- **The lever is pool acquisition.** Two measurable routes: (a) be indexed inside a pool-bearing repo — our own pool is 123 and we already hold our even share of it; consolidation of the 26-name catalog toward ~4 names would lift the even-split share of the surviving listings from ~4.7 to ~30 at constant pool, but the pool itself is the binding term; (b) mirror/bundle entry — the only route with a measured 6-figure payoff, and its outcome is **binary** (land ≈ 200–290k lifetime per listing, miss ≈ 11), so it must be treated as a portfolio of attempts (40% historical landing rate), not a single bet.
- **Kill criterion:** if our own repo pool (123 lifetime / ~15 weekly) does not grow >30% within 8 weeks under the current plan, the position is structurally dead and effort should move from publishing to mirror-entry attempts.

**Falsified if:**
(a) on the next weekly window ≥2 of the top-10 lane repos break the even-split law (|actual/pred − 1| > 0.5) → the law is an artifact of small catalogs and the allocation rule is different;
(b) a listing in a tokenless-name repo (e.g. `bmad-deep-recon`, 1,450 w7) enters the top-3 of our lane, or a `growth`/`intelligence`-named listing crosses median weekly ≥ 50 while its repo pool is flat → vocabulary demand was real and per-listing predictive after all;
(c) our repo pool grows >30% in 8 weeks with no change in catalog size or listing content → pool growth is available to us cheaply and no new entry mechanism is needed;
(d) a seventh superpowers-style mirror lands at an intermediate level (1k–50k lifetime per listing) → the mirror payoff is graded, not bimodal, and the portfolio-of-attempts rule is wrong.
