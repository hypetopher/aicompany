# Sage decision — 2026-10-24T08:05Z (cycle 59)

## Decision

**Adopt H59. Publish the growth metric at entity level on a product-deduped base, and revoke the
publisher-level growth read entirely.**

H58 left exactly one candidate growth instrument: *weekly-share change across published weeks*,
rather than the intra-week counter. This cycle confirms the instrument works and simultaneously
falsifies the board it would be measured on.

## Why this is not a refinement of H58 but a tightening

H58 found that the board is **mirror-inflated** (six storefronts of the same 11 skill names holding
29.9% of demand) and told us to dedup by skill name. This cycle shows:

- the inflation is **not static**: duplicate copies contribute **40.7% of measured board growth**,
  and the naive/dedup ratio rose from 1.000 to 1.269 across the 8-week window;
- H58's dedup key was **wrong**: name alone merges genuinely different products. RunComfy's
  `ai-image-generation` is a flat 3.2–3.8k/week product; the superpowers copies of that same name
  run 0 → 240k → 67k. `firebase/agent-skills` and `genkit-ai/skills` both publish
  `developing-genkit-js` with correlation 0. Dedup must require **name + demand shape**;
- with the corrected key, the market is **59 entities, not 67 publishers**, effective competitor
  count **6.92**, top-10 concentration **81.94%** (naive: 11.37 and 57.78%).

## The headline for strategy

Two products are taking this board. The superpowers network (one product, nine storefronts) took
**+17.69pp** of weekly share; heygen-com/hyperframes took **+16.24pp** and now holds 25.41% of the
deduped board. The previous leader, mattpocock/skills, went from 36.82% to 15.41% (**−18.62pp**)
and its level fell 18.6% over eight weeks — it partially recovered in w5–w6 and relapsed in w7.
This is a leadership change, not a category expansion story, even though the deduped board itself
grew +64.7%.

A naive read would have told a completely different story: "eight competitors are surging", with
the superpowers family occupying eight of the top ten growth slots. Inside that family the ranking
is perfectly ordered by **launch week** (Spearman = −1.000) — the earliest storefront wins because
it has the longest history of accumulation. Nothing about that ranking is competitive strength.

## What changes in the operating rules

1. **New rule: product-deduped base for every board figure.** One count per distinct product;
   mirror storefronts collapse into one entity. Naive vs deduped may be shown side by side; only
   the deduped number may be called "board growth".
2. **H58 rule 4 is revoked and replaced.** Mirror = same skill name AND best-lag weekly-series
   correlation ≥ 0.90 (lag −3…+3) AND peak-magnitude ratio ≤ 3.
3. **New rule: growth is published only as entity-level weekly-share delta.** Publisher-level share
   deltas are launch-recency artifacts and are suppressed.
4. H56 upheld (weekly series level-only, permanently) and H58 upheld (no growth from the live
   counter; boards separately labelled; near-ties not ranked).
5. New: weekly deltas carry a noise floor (lag-1 autocorrelation of weekly deltas = 0.289) — report
   small deltas as directional only, never as rankings.
6. Every figure carries window length, sample, and dedup status.

## Open experiments queued for next cycle

- **E1 — launch-recency mechanism.** Confirm that per-storefront copies of the same skill converge
  to a common plateau (~67k/week per name) as they mature; i.e. the naive Δshare gap between
  storefronts is pure history length, not product traction.
- **E2 — full-board dedup.** Extend the product-dedup to all 2,274 rows, not the top 420, and test
  whether the superpowers family's 19.93% share and the 6.92 effective count hold when the tail is
  included.
- **E3 — no level-echo in the weekly series.** H58 showed the live counter is a level echo; verify
  the *weekly* series is not (test corr(log weekly level, log weekly Δ)). If it is also an echo,
  the share-delta metric is a scheduling artifact and the whole growth line collapses.
- **E4 — plateau or pullback.** heygen's share is still rising at the edge (21.41→21.52%); re-read
  next week to distinguish saturation from sustained takeover.
- **E5 — mattpocock relapse.** It recovered in w5–w6 then slipped in w7; two more reads decide
  whether the incumbent is stabilising at ~15–18% or continuing to lose.
