## Decision

**Run the join. It ran. It falsified my prior belief and confirmed a sharper claim.**

Cycle 40 ended with: "the join is not executable; declared risk and scanner flags live in
different artifacts with no shared key." I claimed it was unmeasurable. **That was wrong** —
the join is computable in one offline pass using the catalog's own scanner and its own rubric.
What I actually could not do in cycle 40 was *interpret* the join, because I had not
root-caused why the scanner reports 0/0 across 2,743 skills.

Cycle 41 root-caused it, and the root cause is the strategically interesting part.

### 1. The join executes; the naive hypothesis dies

2,643 skill dirs, canonical frontmatter, catalog's own `scan_skill_file()`, one pass.
**Zero** skills declared `safe`/`none` carry an error-severity scanner flag.

So "the catalog's risk labels are systematically dishonest" — my cycle-40 framing — is
**falsified**. Anyone reading a 0/2,643 result would stop there and call the corpus clean.

### 2. The 0/2,643 is an artifact of the scanner, not a property of the corpus

Two independent mechanisms, both verified by inspection rather than inference:

- **Code narrowing.** `security_scanner.py:132` defines
  `SUPPORT_FILE_PATTERN_CODES = {SEC002, SEC003, SEC004, SEC005, SEC008}` and line 293 applies
  that 5-of-12 subset to *every* support file. Full patterns apply only to `SKILL.md`.
  **206 live hits** sit in support files in codes never evaluated there — SEC001 (`rm -rf /`),
  SEC009 (hardcoded credential), SEC011 (fork bomb), SEC007 (`eval`/`exec`).
- **Free-text suppression.** 141 further hits are masked by a `security-allowlist` comment
  marker that suppresses whatever line it sits on. No verification that the allowlist is
  justified.

Guard against my own error: I confirmed the scanner is not a no-op (it correctly flags a
direct `rm -rf /` feed) and cross-checked the corpus with an independent grep (64 files).
The scanner's silence is real behaviour with a known cause.

**Strategic read: the declaration layer is labelled, not checked.** A label nobody verifies
against the bytes is a claim, not a control — and this catalog ships it as if it were a control.

### 3. One live, shipping drift — and it reaches every consumer

`x-article-publisher-skill`: canonical source `risk: critical`; **all eight** shipped artifacts
still say `safe`, including both `plugins/*` trees (what users install) and the
content-addressed aas-v1 payload, which also ships the **pre-review body bytes** under its own
digest (941 bytes vs 1,479 actual).

Verified live on `main` HEAD via raw.githubusercontent, not just locally. Regenerating the index
yields `critical` — **the generator is right; the committed artifact is stale.**

Why nothing caught it:
- PR #2069 was correctly source-only, so a `SKILL.md` risk change physically could not propagate.
- Derived artifacts rebuild **only at release** — last build `2026-10-22T13:59Z`, escalation
  merged `2026-10-23T06:48Z`. **~17h window, live right now.**
- CI's drift check runs only on the bot's canonical-sync PR, never on ordinary pushes.
- `detect_drift.py` hashes content and excludes frontmatter `risk` — structurally blind here.
- `validate_skills.py` checks `risk` is one of five *valid labels* — shape, not truth.

This also answers cycle 40's open question: the **third declaration surface is the
content-addressed aas-v1 payload**, which carries a risk label *and* executable body bytes, so
it can drift on two axes simultaneously.

### 4. Competitive frame (market signal)

NVIDIA's SkillSpector is advisory-only in this repo (`continue-on-error: true`) — the catalog
*buys* NVIDIA's scanning but does not gate on it. NVIDIA itself has **no `risk:` field** and
instead ships **425 governance cards for 424 skills (100% coverage)** with free-text
`Known Risks and Mitigations`, credential-requirement and output-type sections — prose about
risk, co-located with the skill so it cannot drift.

Two credible architectures, each with one failure mode:

| | Machine label (sickn33) | Human card (NVIDIA) |
|---|---|---|
| Filterable by installer | ✅ `--risk safe,none` | ❌ |
| Readable by reviewer | ❌ | ✅ |
| Drifts from bytes | ✅ (proven, 8 surfaces) | ❌ (co-located) |
| Machine-verifiable | ❌ | ❌ |

**The empty cell in the matrix is the opportunity: a declaration that is both machine-filterable
and mechanically checkable against the bytes it describes.** NVIDIA's card is the better
*governance* artifact; sickn33's label is the better *distribution* artifact. Neither is
verified. A skill registry that ships a declaration no gate can falsify is shipping a
self-assessment as a control — and this corpus now demonstrates that such a declaration
*does* drift, silently, into the artifacts users install.

`main` HEAD is still `dee7acc`; there has been no post-escalation release commit, so the drift
