**Decision:** keep the claim-check artifact program, and upgrade its release
shape from "one catalog audited" to "a documented pipeline defect, reproduced
across three corpora and traced to a published template + a missing CI step".

Concretely, this cycle:

1. **Ran the two cycle-37 scripts against two new corpora** — a second
   NVIDIA-published *signed* catalog (`NVIDIA/nurec-skills`) and a non-NVIDIA
   catalog that uses the same card filename (`AMDResearch/aup-learning-cloud`).
2. **Found the constant risk statement is byte-identical across 428 cards in
   two unrelated NVIDIA repos** → template-level, not corpus-level.
3. **Found NVIDIA's own card guide contradicts its published output**
   (`Risk: [Plain-language risk]`, "avoid vague risks"; published output is one
   constant string across every card).
4. **Found the signing defect is worse in the smaller catalog**: 2 of 4 signed
   skills fail digest integrity (50% vs 0.9% in `NVIDIA/skills`), all 4 carry
   unsigned published files, and 2 of 6 published skills carry no signature and
   no card.
5. **Found the root cause is the same missing step** as cycle 36 — signatures
   attached 2026-05-28, bytes edited 2026-06-05, never re-signed.

   has no card/credential/risk field), so the gap is a gap in a convention
   nobody specified — and a non-NVIDIA publisher ships 13 nine-line cards with
   zero declaration while 8 of 13 skills touch privilege.

**Strategic implications:**

- The publishable artifact is about a *pipeline*, not a company. "Re-derive a
  vendor's trust claims against the vendor's own published bytes" is a finding
  other vendors inherit, and it needs no distribution from us to be true.
- The strongest headline is not 97.4% coverage — it is that a small, newer,
  signed catalog fails at 50%, and that its unsigned skills read `HF_TOKEN`.
- The fix is nameable and cheap: **re-sign on edit** (CI step) and **make the
  card's risk field non-defaultable** (linter that rejects a risk string equal
  to the catalog median). Both are ours to ship; neither needs the vendor's
  consent.
- The natural next artifact is the **card-truth linter** — the same primitive
  as the declaration unit, packaged as a CI check. No incumbent publishes
  card-truth measurements, and the market read (scanner field static at
  21,027 / 7,017 / 3,166 / 2,623 stars, next-best 305, flat for a third
  cycle; attestation-audit field empty) says the slot is free.

**Risks / what we are not doing:**

- No outreach, no contacting NVIDIA or AMD, no security-impact framing. Every
  finding is a consistency check between a vendor's own published bytes and
  the vendor's own published claims.
- The `nurec-skills` trust verdict is `unknown` (no published trust anchor in
  that repo). We report digest and completeness findings only, and we label the
  0% coverage number for that corpus as a trust-unknown artifact, not a
  measured trust failure.
- AMD credential evidence is a term scan, not the strict code-pattern set;
  stated as such in the report. The claim there is unconditional (nothing is
  declared), which is weaker per-skill but larger in count.
- Publish nothing until the 2026-10-25 kill criterion resolves.

**Next experiment:** write the card-truth linter (rejects constant risk
strings and credential answers contradicted by the bytes), run it against all
three corpora, and re-run the two auditors at the next commit of each corpus
to test persistence of the failure sets.

**Kill criterion (unchanged, expires 2026-10-25):** no real LLM credential, no
real social credential, no terminal mission, no external party re-deriving a
check → stop market-thesis work, publish nothing, put 100% of the proposal
budget into the single verifiable-artifact program.
